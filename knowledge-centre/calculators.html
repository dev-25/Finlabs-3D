Behind the numbers

How the maths works

SIP

A Systematic Investment Plan puts the same amount in every month. Each instalment earns a return for the months that follow it, so the earliest ones do the most work.

Total = P × [ (1 + i)n − 1 ] ÷ i × (1 + i)

P is the monthly amount, i the monthly rate (the yearly rate ÷ 12) and n the number of instalments. Each instalment is counted from the start of its month.

Lumpsum

A lumpsum is one amount invested once and left alone. It compounds on itself every year, which is why the curve starts slowly and steepens later.

Total = P × (1 + r)t

P is the amount invested, r the yearly rate and t the number of years.

Which one?

A SIP spreads the buying across months, so the price you pay averages out and the habit does the saving for you. A lumpsum puts the whole amount to work from day one, which helps when you already hold the money — and hurts more if markets fall soon after.

Many plans use both: a lumpsum for what you have today, a SIP for what you earn each month.

These figures are illustrations, not promises. They assume a steady rate of return every year, and real markets do not work that way. They leave out taxes, exit loads and fund charges, and nothing here is investment advice — talk to your advisor before you commit money.
